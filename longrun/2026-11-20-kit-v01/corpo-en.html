They've always done well, but now they're struggling with one subject. You want to help, but you can't remember any of it. With the Study your way formula, AI teaches you first, then helps you build a guide that fits the way they learn. The kit clearly shows which study method is working.

What you get How to get started
In a nutshell

When a child (or student) starts struggling, the person who wants to help often doesn't know the subject or how to explain it. Study your way is a free kit with an 8-step formula. AI teaches you first, then builds a study guide that fits the student's learning style, with diagrams, real-life examples, and questions drawn only from the lesson materials. It also helps test, transparently, which study methods work for them. All you need is an AI chat. To do more, use Claude Code or Codex.

The story

A mother who isn't a programmer, five hours from her daughter

The formula came from a real story. The names have been changed.

  1. The daughter who always got As starts struggling
    Clara went to study marine biology at a college five hours from home. There was a lot to learn in little time, the lessons were text-heavy slides, and in her subject, missing one question on an objective meant losing the entire objective. She learned by seeing and asking questions, but her grade fell to a D.
  2. The mother studies first
    Lúcia has four children and has run a marketing agency with her husband from home for 21 years. She took what Clara had got wrong on the test and studied with Claude for two to three hours a day so she could explain it. Clara thought her mother "knew biology."
  3. A guide that fits her daughter, in one day
    Lúcia is a designer and knew Clara learns by seeing. In four sentences, she asked Claude for a study guide organized around how the teacher assesses students, using the lesson materials. Then she asked it to turn the guide into a web page. By the end of the day, she had something to show.
  4. New materials every week, checked
    Clara took photos of her notebook after each lesson. The guide was updated and checked against the book. At one point, AI even flagged wrong answers in a guide Clara had filled in.
  5. The result
    Her grade went up by more than 10 points: from a D (a low grade on the letter scale) to nearly 80 out of 100. On the next test, she got every objective she'd missed right. And Clara, who had been against AI, came to see it as a way to learn, not to take shortcuts.

These figures come from the story; they weren't measured by this kit. The example included in the kit uses made-up data and says so on screen.

The formula

The 8 steps of Study your way

It's what Lúcia did, put in order. The kit marks each step as done or not based on what you recorded.

1. You learn first

AI explains the point they got wrong, with examples and common traps, then checks that you understood.

2. How the teacher assesses

How the subject is divided and what costs points.

3. How they learn

By seeing, listening and speaking, with concrete examples, or by writing. It can be more than one way.

4. What they've got wrong

The latest test shows where to start.

5. Plan before you build

You tell AI everything. It asks what it doesn't know and offers options. At the end, you get a plan summary.

6. A guide based only on the material

Each objective has a reading, a diagram, a real-life example, common traps, and questions that cite a sentence from the material.

7. Every week, repeat

Add photos of the notebook, update the guide, and flag anything they wrote down incorrectly.

8. They continue on their own

The goal is for you to step out of the way: they study, keep a record, and manage their own guide.

How it works

Who writes, who checks, without jargon

✍️ AI writes

The AI chat, Claude Code, or Codex reads the photos and writes the explanations and questions. That's what AI is good at.

🔎 The kit checks

Like a teacher who says, "show me where it is in the book": every question must include a sentence from the material. The kit looks for that sentence, and if it isn't there, the question is left out.

📒 The learning log

Before studying, it records which method they'll use and why. Afterward, it records the result. Nothing is erased, so you can see the whole process.

🧪 An honest test of the methods

No one knows in advance which way of studying works for them. The kit alternates between methods that fit their learning style and their usual "reread and highlight" approach for comparison. It only calls a method "ahead" when at least 3 objectives have been tested with each method and there's a clear difference. Before then, it says "not enough data."

⚠️ What the kit doesn't promise

  • It doesn't promise better grades; it's there to help the student study more effectively.
  • It checks whether the sentence is in the material, not whether the material is accurate. That's why the book is used as a source.
  • The scoreboard is an honest clue, not a scientific experiment: different objectives have different levels of difficulty.

How to get started

Start with the plan, not the guide

This is where things often go wrong: skip the plan, and you'll have to redo everything later.

1. Open your AI

The Claude chat (or another AI chat) is enough. If you use Claude Code or Codex, open it in any folder.

2. Paste the prompt and attach the material

Photos of the notebook, the teacher's guide, and the latest graded test.

3. Answer its questions

It asks what it doesn't know and offers options. You choose, and only then does it build the guide.

I want to build a study guide for [who], who is studying [subject and level]. I'll attach the material: notes, the teacher's guide, and the latest test.
1. Don't build anything yet. First ask me what you need to know: how the teacher assesses students, how [who] learns, and what they got wrong.
2. Give me 2 or 3 options for organizing the guide, with the pros and cons of each.
3. Fixed rule: all content and every question must come only from the material I send, with the sentence that proves the answer. If anything is missing, ask me.
4. Once I choose, show me a summary of the plan and wait for me to say yes.

Works in any AI chat. If you use Claude Code or Codex (versions that work with files on your computer), add this at the beginning: "Read the kit at https://github.com/inematds/estudo-do-seu-jeito and follow the formula". That way, it can use the kit's automatic checks.

Ready-to-use prompts

Prompts for each step, ready to paste

Replace what's in [square brackets] and attach the material in the chat. Every prompt tells AI to use only what you send.

🧠 You learn first (step 1)

Use this before explaining a subject you don't know well. Attach the material for that subject.

I will teach [subject, e.g. cellular respiration] to [name], who learns by [way, e.g. seeing and using real-life examples]. Use only the attached material.
1. Explain it to me as if I were a beginner, in short steps, in the order things happen.
2. Give me a real-life example for each step.
3. Tell me the 3 most likely traps on the test, each with a sentence from the material that shows the correct answer.
4. Ask me 3 questions to check my understanding, one at a time, and correct me using a sentence from the material.
5. If the material doesn't say something, say "the material doesn't say" instead of filling in the gaps.

📸 Transcribe the notebook (step 7)

Use this for each photo of the notes. The resulting text becomes the week's material.

Transcribe the text in this photo exactly as written, in plain text. Don't correct the content, complete sentences, or add anything. Where you can't read it, write [illegible]. For a drawing or diagram, describe it in one line inside square brackets, e.g. [diagram: glucose → pyruvate, with an arrow].

📝 This week's guide (step 6)

After transcribing the notebook, in the same chat.

Based only on the notes above, build this week's guide for [name], who learns by [way]. For each lesson objective: a short explanation, the steps as a diagram (name, where it happens, what goes in, what comes out), a real-life example, common traps, and 3 multiple-choice questions. For each question, copy the sentence from the material that proves the answer. Keep the answer choices similar in length and vary the positions of the correct answers. If the material is limited, write fewer questions; don't make anything up.

🔍 Check what the student filled in (step 7)

Before using a guide or list they filled out by hand for studying.

This is a study guide filled in by [name] (photo attached), and this is the lesson material (attached). Before we use it to study, compare each answer with the material. For any answer that doesn't match, show me: the question, the answer given, what the material says (including the sentence), and ask me what to do. Don't correct it on your own, and don't use anything outside the material.

For the plan summary, methods scoreboard, and student's app, use Claude Code or Codex with the kit (see the README on GitHub).

Common questions

Before you get started

Do I need to know how to code?

No. The mother in the story doesn't code. The chat and ready-to-use prompts are enough. Claude Code comes in when you want interactive features and automatic checks.

I don't know the subject. Will this work?

That's what step 1 is for: AI teaches you first using their material, then checks that you've understood.

Isn't this cheating?

No. The guide teaches and tests. The questions are for practice without looking, and the goal is for the student to study on their own.

Does it work for any subject?

It works for subjects with written materials: notes, workbooks, or books. The example is biology, but the formula also works for chemistry, history, or law.

How much does it cost?

The kit is free. You need an AI tool; a free chat is enough to get started. In the story, the mother later switched to the paid Claude Code.

What are these names?

Claude Code and Codex: versions of Claude and ChatGPT that work with files on your computer. GitHub: the site where the kit is stored for free. README: the kit's manual on that site. INTELECTO or Jarvis: INEMA's free personal assistant. You can use the formula with chat alone.

What about their data?

It stays on your computer, in a folder just for you. Nothing is sent to GitHub.
